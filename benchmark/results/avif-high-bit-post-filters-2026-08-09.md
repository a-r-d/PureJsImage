# AVIF high-bit post-filter decode evidence (2026-08-09)

## Scope

This result expands the pinned high-bit AVIF decode boundary with nine lossy single-frame fixtures:

- 10-bit YUV 4:2:0 with deblocking, CDEF, and Wiener restoration;
- 10-bit YUV 4:2:2 with CDEF and Wiener restoration;
- limited-range 10-bit YUV 4:2:0 with deblocking, CDEF, and self-guided restoration;
- 10-bit YUV 4:4:4 with deblocking and self-guided restoration;
- 12-bit YUV 4:2:0 with deblocking, CDEF, and Wiener restoration;
- 12-bit YUV 4:2:0 with deblocking, CDEF, and self-guided restoration; and
- 12-bit YUV 4:2:0, 4:2:2, and 4:4:4 with deblocking and CDEF.

The existing TypeScript reconstruction and post-filter kernels required no new branch: the new fixtures prove that their depth-scaled arithmetic already matches independent native decoders for these syntax combinations.

## Fixture provenance

`filtered-lossy-10bpc-yuv420-192x128.avif`, `filtered-lossy-10bpc-yuv422-64x64.avif`, and the three compact 12-bit fixtures use deterministic high-frequency Y4M sources generated by `prepare-high-bit-expanded-fixtures.ts`. `self-guided-10bpc-yuv420-320x192.avif` uses a deterministic 320x192 crop of the checksum-pinned `fox.profile0.8bpc.yuv420.avif` corpus image, converted to limited-range 10-bit YUV 4:2:0. The 10-bit YUV 4:4:4 self-guided fixture and both 12-bit restoration fixtures use the same crop converted to their pinned native-depth Y4M formats.

The original six AVIF files were encoded with libavif `avifenc` 1.3.0 and libaom 3.12.1. The three added restoration fixtures were encoded through FFmpeg 7.1.1 with libaom 3.12.1 in deterministic still-picture mode. Source and output checksums and encoder settings are part of `high-bit-expanded-fixtures.ts`.

## Isolated memory evidence

`npm run bench:avif:memory -- --output benchmark/results/avif-memory-high-depth-2026-08-09.json` ran three checksum-pinned isolated cold processes for each scenario. The 1204x800 sources use compatible filtered 10-bit and 12-bit YUV 4:2:0 frames; the downscale workflows resize to 301x200 and encode PNG.

| Workflow | Median peak RSS delta | Median sampled external delta | Median sampled ArrayBuffer delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10-bit 1204x800 decode | 15.43 MiB | 7.67 MiB | 7.47 MiB |
| 10-bit 1204x800 to 301x200 PNG | 14.30 MiB | 6.87 MiB | 6.71 MiB |
| 12-bit 1204x800 decode | 19.43 MiB | 7.67 MiB | 7.47 MiB |
| 12-bit 1204x800 to 301x200 PNG | 16.68 MiB | 6.87 MiB | 6.81 MiB |

These filtered high-depth paths emit RGBA in bounded 32-row blocks, but reconstruction and post-filtering retain padded full-frame native-depth YUV. Downscaling therefore avoids a source-sized RGBA output but does not reduce the native-YUV reconstruction class. This is an explicit full-frame fallback, not evidence that filtered high-depth decode preserves the filter-free bounded-row architecture.

## Boundary

Evidence covers only the nine pinned frame-header, entropy, reconstruction, and filter combinations above. It does not claim every legal high-depth AV1 post-filter combination. Loop restoration on 10-bit or 12-bit YUV 4:2:2 and 12-bit YUV 4:4:4 remains unvalidated. High bit depth does not imply PQ, HLG, HDR output, or wide-gamut conversion support. The decoder still emits 8-bit RGBA after retaining native-depth YUV through reconstruction and filtering.
